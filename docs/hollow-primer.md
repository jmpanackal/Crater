# The Hollow Primer

*A one-read orientation to Krater's story canon, for a collaborator coming in fresh. Not itself a source of truth — if anything here ever conflicts with [`mechanics-canon.md`](mechanics-canon.md) or [`story.md`](story.md), those win. Updated 2026-09-15.*

**Read this once, straight through — nothing here depends on knowing anything else.** The glossary at the end works as a lookup afterward. The closing section tells you which threads are already decided versus which ones are genuinely still open to shape.

## The premise

Generations ago, a colony ship broke apart during its crash on an alien moon. The main mass tore open a massive crater and became **the Hollow** — a society built into and around the wound. A smaller section came down safer on the surface, with fewer survivors. Neither side knows the other lived. Each has spent generations assuming the other died.

The Hollow is built around **the Devil's Mouth**, the crater itself — genuinely dangerous, no myth required. The opposite direction is sacred and forbidden: **the Firmament**, the rock overhead. The taboo against digging up is backed by a real disaster, distorted into doctrine over generations. The player is a kid who never bought the Firmament story — not a rebel, just someone whose curiosity points up while everyone else's points down into the Mouth.

## Act One — Underground
*Fully locked. This is what's actually being built right now.*

Daily life in the Hollow runs on three production districts — Glowbeds, Wickwork, Cistern — a small **Council of Stewards**, and a civic rhythm set by **the Pulse**, a degraded ship relic at Mid Heart, ending each cycle in **Ritual**. Working with old salvaged technology isn't folk magic sitting beside religion — it *is* religion, called **Divine Binding**, practiced by **Pulse Binders** based at the Pulse and radiating out into the districts.

**Albus Socul**, the First Steward, gives the player their first real job: help excavate a new civic district, which secretly conceals a route to his own private medical supply. He reads as genuinely helpful in Act 1, not villainous.

### The founding myth

The Hollow's foundational story is **Heavenfall** — plainly, "the day the sky fell." Generations ago an expedition dug upward and broke into water above the Firmament, causing a real collapse and flood that killed people. Albus was there, possibly leading it. Doctrine reframed the tragedy afterward as divine judgment on a "selfish, questioning generation" — the disaster was real; the moral framing on top of it is what's manufactured. Cistern's water traces back to that same flood, pooled at the bottom of the Devil's Mouth ever since.

### Climbing, and what it costs

Through civic work the player earns Tallies and Trust, climbing the housing ladder: **Lower → the Mid Reach → Ashram Heights**. Privately, they start diverting district goods and — once they reach the Mid Reach — grafting recovered technology into their own body: **Hellbinding** (also called Voidbinding), practiced by **Hell Binders**/**Void Binders**. It's the same root idea as Pulse Binding — joining technology to something — just joined to a person instead of a tool, which is exactly the line doctrine forbids. The gear itself is real: fragments of equipment built for **the Firstwalkers**, the colony ship's own pioneer corps, never meant for ordinary colonists. Folklore holds that going too far "traps your soul in the void" — probably superstition, possibly not entirely.

### Where secrecy actually happens

Secret work isn't defined by direction, it's defined by sanction. Public digging is always lateral, at an authorized front, supervised and reported. Unauthorized digging can happen anywhere off that path — an unreported side-pocket branching off an official gallery, a probe toward (not into) the Devil's Mouth, or, carrying the heaviest weight of all, upward into the Firmament, because Heavenfall gives that one direction real religious consequence the others don't share. A curious player can find real secret content — including the wreckage of the original Heavenfall expedition itself, tucked inside the Firmament dig as an optional discovery — well before they ever reach Ashram Heights.

One authored personal cost lands before Act 1 ends — a friend covers for the player, or is hurt by a lie. Act 1 closes with the Firmament finally breached and the surface revealed: a total surprise, with no hint given beforehand that there's more world than the Hollow.

## Act Two — The Breach
*Outline-level. Not being built yet — kept consistent so Act 1 doesn't paint it into a corner.*

The surface is **the Reef** — vivid and alien, not literally underwater but dense-atmosphere enough to look that way. First revelation: the "collapse" doctrine exaggerated was real, just not what anyone was told.

The player builds a surface settlement, recruiting Hollow residents by Trust. They also find the other half of the original crash — the smaller ship section's survivors, who've assumed the Hollow died the same way the Hollow assumed they had. Reuniting lands as "the dead aren't dead" for both sides at once, not a reunion with people who chose to leave. That group is smaller and resource-poor — they never had access to the crash site's material economy — but the player's own tools and knowledge can help rebuild them. Devil's Mouth descent continues in parallel, deeper than Act 1 ever allowed.

## Act Three — The Truth
*Outline-level. Several pieces genuinely still open — see below.*

The crash was a tragedy, not an attack: the native species shot the ship down out of fear. The wreck is still down at the bottom of the Devil's Mouth, submerged — a sufficiently developed Cistern is the plausible way to eventually drain it and reach it.

Albus is revealed as Heavenfall's architect: present at the original disaster, doctrine born from real guilt and a genuine, if self-serving, attempt to prevent it happening again, curdled over decades of isolation and sustained use of the same restricted Firstwalker technology that's kept him alive. Publicly the Hollow's greatest Pulse Binder; privately its deepest Hell/Void Binder, hoarding everything that could reveal the truth.

The sky's "guiding light" is the real homeworld. Reaching the true bottom of the Mouth yields what's needed to build an escape ship. The native species offer a fight-or-peace choice, resolved without a combat system — "fight" is one authored tense set-piece, not a parallel game. Peace opens them as a third recruitment source alongside the Hollow and the reunited surface survivors.

The ending runs on two axes, not one: Trust and recruitment decide **who leaves and how many**; build progression — how developed Cistern is, which playstyle the player leaned into, how far into Hellbinding they went — decides **what's reachable, and what happens to the player specifically**.

## A working glossary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **The Hollow** | The underground society, built into and around the Devil's Mouth. Also what everyone just calls "home." |
| **The Devil's Mouth** | The central crater void. Also called the Mouth, the Deep, the Void, or — among the fearful — Hell. |
| **The Firmament** | The sacred ceiling. Never dig up — genuinely thick, not a shortcut. |
| **Heavenfall** | "The day the sky fell." The real disaster behind the Firmament taboo — Albus was there. |
| **Divine Binding** | The Hollow's actual religion: sacred, ritual work with inherited technology. |
| **Pulse Binders** | Sanctioned practitioners of Divine Binding, based at the Pulse. |
| **Hellbinding / Voidbinding** | The forbidden version of Divine Binding — joining technology to a body instead of a tool. |
| **The Firstwalkers** | The colony ship's pre-crash pioneer corps. Their gear is what makes Hellbinding possible. |
| **Trust** | Society's qualitative opinion of the player's reliability. Not a spendable currency. |
| **Lower / the Mid Reach / Ashram Heights** | The three residence tiers the player climbs across Act 1. |
| **The Pulse / Ritual** | The degraded ship relic that sets the Hollow's civic clock, and the daily gathering that closes each cycle. |
| **Albus Socul** | The First Steward. Helpful in Act 1; revealed in Act 3 as Heavenfall's architect. |
| **The Reef** | The alien surface, revealed at the end of Act 1. Not literally underwater — the air is just that dense. |

## What's settled, and what's actually yours to shape

Everything in **Act One** above is locked — treat it as fixed ground, not a draft. Acts Two and Three are the outline, and several real pieces inside them are still open. These are the honest ones to bring ideas to:

- **Open — surface threats.** Whether danger on the Reef is wildlife, environmental hazards, or both — undecided.
- **Open — how deep the alien-contact choice goes.** Current lean is a binary fight-or-peace with a small unlockable branch, not a full second faction — but not locked.
- **Open — Albus's endgame.** Exposed, defeated, escapes, or some mix depending on player choices — genuinely undecided.
- **Open — villain's precise psychological arc.** The Heavenfall connection and the "protector curdled into controller" throughline are locked; the exact mechanism of how the Firstwalker technology affected him is not.
- **Locked — everything about Act 1** above: the Firmament, Heavenfall, Divine Binding, the housing ladder, Albus's role, the Breach. Don't propose alternatives to these without flagging it as a real conversation, not a quick pitch.
- **Locked — the crash's true cause** (first-contact tragedy, not malice), the **two reunited survivor populations**, and the **two-axis ending** (Trust/recruitment + build progression). These can be built on, not overwritten.
