## Canon — Act 1 fiction

### World as people know it

- Society lives in and around a massive shaft — **the Pit** — torn open ages ago: cliffside terraces and bridges ring a deep central void (see `docs/refs/hollow_concept.png`). Truth of the crash has rotted into myth; everyone has a theory about how deep it goes; nobody who’s gone far enough has come back to settle it.
- Working name for the whole world-as-known: **the Hollow**. Day-to-day speech should prefer **named subsections** (districts, tunnels, chambers), not constant “the Hollow” (see decisions #1–#2, #28).
- The sacred ceiling is **the Firmament** in formal speech; most people call it the Vault or simply the roof. The Firmament is believed to hold only through shared work, obedience, and grace.
- The Hollow's foundational correction myth says an earlier generation hoarded, questioned its duties, and looked upward; the Firmament collapsed on them while the faithful were spared. This story makes contribution a moral duty and gives the taboo against upward digging real social force.
- Unbreakable rule the *other* way: **never dig upward**. The ceiling myth is manufactured control. The Pit is *genuinely* dangerous without needing a myth — that asymmetry is load-bearing tone. The player should discover that the Firmament doctrine is false or deliberately distorted, not that the Pit is harmless.
- Title **Krater** is also diegetic: the pit *is* the crater the ship carved (players don’t get that confirmation in Act 1).

### Protagonist

- A kid who never fully bought the Firmament story. Not a rebel — curiosity just points **up**, while everyone else’s wonder points **down**.
- They begin in the working, lower terraces. By helping the Hollow thrive, they earn higher residence, responsibility, and eventually access to Vaultward: the upper maintenance terraces immediately beneath the Firmament. Advancement brings privilege and a better home, but also scrutiny, duties, and more to lose.
- Secret upward digging begins only once the player can work from Vaultward. Same tools can also push into the Pit’s upper walls — public-ish curiosity, dangerous if you go too far — first hint the two mysteries share a root.

### Life underground

- **Production districts** — named areas with distinct communal outputs; efficiency / “safe magic” upgrades raise their passive rates (#28). The Act 1 production triad is intentionally compact: glowbeds grow food and fiber, wickworks make lamps/rope/tool bindings, and the deep cistern supplies water and pressure power. Current names remain placeholders until permanent fiction names are locked.
- **Harvest** — communal rhythm and accountability clock on top of that economy; miss it and you’re noticed.
- **Living Hollow** — people work, live, play, and talk. Home base must feel inhabited (#28).
- The Hollow is warm and mutually dependent, but carries the undertones of a practical theocracy. People vary: some genuinely believe the Firmament doctrine, some comply for safety or status, and some quietly joke about it. Light folk comedy remains: belly-of-a-beast theories, nursery rhymes, one person who says it is all just rock.

### The First Steward

- The future antagonist is publicly the **First Steward**: the trusted authority over production, ritual, and sanctioned “magic.” They are useful, calm, and genuinely capable of improving life in the Hollow.
- The player first meets the Steward through an official assignment: excavate a whole **new district**. This teaches public digging, communal production, and the social value of contributing before secrecy enters the loop.
- The district should initially have a plausible civic purpose — expansion, storage, new glowbeds, or a pressure annex. Later evidence reveals the Steward selected its location for an ominous reason connected to the buried ship, old infrastructure, or control of access. The exact hidden purpose remains open; do not lock it until it serves the final Act 1/Act 3 plan.
- The Steward does not read as openly villainous in Act 1. Their later reveal is that they preserved control by turning emergency knowledge and old-world restrictions into doctrine.

### Magicians (lore → systems)

- Rote use of old salvaged machinery is called **magic**. The future villain encourages that framing and likely poses as the most gifted magician (#25).
- **Sanctioned / “safe magic”** (mundane tools) → efficiency upgrades, open district boosts.
- **Forbidden / “dangerous magic”** (nav, logs, legible data) → knowledge upgrades, risk / Standing.
- Act 1 players only feel “some magic is common, some is rare/suspicious.”

### Fragments (stay ambiguous)

- Seeded up and down; readable as myth, religion, or history — **never** clear “we’re on an alien planet” in Act 1.
- **Recovered salvage** → mechanical work-rig upgrades. It includes metal, wire, glass, seals, and old components, and is useful only after it is carried home.
- **District supplies** → tangible communal inputs gathered on expeditions: fungal nutrients/spores for glowbeds, conductive metal and fibers for wickworks, and mineral salts, pump parts, or pressure seals for the cistern. These support visible passive production projects rather than a second complex economy.
- **Records / knowledge fragments** → lore and knowledge upgrades (always forbidden-tier). A fragment must do more than add a Journal entry: it should unlock a tool junction, reveal a hidden route/seam, or contradict a piece of Firmament doctrine.

### Secrecy systems (story meaning)

- **Siphon** — personal work-rig upgrades divert communal effort/materials; spend back home. Healthier district output covers diversion; thin output makes people notice. The player should visibly see districts improve and their passive stocks rise, so helping the Hollow feels good before it becomes cover for private work.
- **Social Standing** — caught digging up, lying, siphoning under thin cover, or using unsanctioned tech costs standing; exposed lies hurt worse than honest catches. Gates later recruitment (seed for Acts 2–3).

### Work-rig progression

- The player improves a personal **work rig**, not their body with abstract stats. In-world, its sanctioned pieces are normal worker equipment; its forbidden pieces are restored or improvised old technology.
- Upgrade families are: digging (force versus quiet), movement/recovery, secrecy, knowledge, and community utility. Major upgrades should be meaningful junctions, not a long list of percentage boosts.
- A movement/recovery tool is required for the game’s vertical spaces. The early form should be a practical **lift rig**: a compact winch, glider-lift, or tether system that lets the player recover from deep Pit routes and remain positioned during upper-Firmament work. It must have constrained fuel/charge, anchor, or cooldown rules so vertical traversal remains a decision rather than free flight.
- The core upgrade tension is deliberate: some upgrades make the player more useful to the Hollow, while others make them better at uncovering its truth.

### Act 1 arc — The Firmament

1. **The Hollow Holds:** The player learns ordinary work, Harvest, the three production districts, public Pit digging, and Firmament doctrine.
2. **The Steward’s District:** The First Steward assigns the player to help excavate a new district. This provides the first public reason to dig, introduces the Steward as helpful authority, and exposes a contradiction or strange fragment.
3. **Earned Ascent:** Communal projects, contribution, and social trust advance the player through the Hollow’s vertical hierarchy toward Vaultward.
4. **The Constructed Seal:** From upper access, the player discovers a maintenance layer behind the Firmament. It is unmistakably made rather than divine, but does not yet explain the colony or surface.
5. **The Cost of Looking:** One authored social consequence makes secrecy personal: a friend covers for the player at cost, is hurt by a lie, or asks them to choose duty over progress. Random Standing checks support this beat but do not replace it.
6. **The Breach:** With a completed work-rig and forbidden knowledge, the player opens the Firmament. The player controls the quiet, enormous reveal of the surface.

### Act 1 design goal (narrative)

**Act 1 should feel like it might be the whole game.** No surface tease, no “more world” UI. Player braces for punishment when digging up. The Pit stays an unexplained lived-with mystery. The breach is Act 1’s final reveal; there is no surface gameplay or surface-facing UI before it.
